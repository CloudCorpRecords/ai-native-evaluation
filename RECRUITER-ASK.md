# The AI-native evaluation ask

Written for Rene Turcios, 2026-11-07. Everything in this file is sendable as-is once the bracketed fields are filled in. The copy names no company and no person, so the same text works with any employer and any assessment vendor.

How to use it:

- Part A is the email. Send it after a recruiter schedules an automated assessment and before taking it.
- Part B is one paragraph for application form fields, LinkedIn messages, and anywhere space is tight.
- Part C is the one-pager. Attach it or paste it when a recruiter wants the longer case, or when a process has already produced a misleading score and needs context.

The stance in all three parts: ask plainly, follow the rules as written if the answer is no, and offer a better measurement instead of arguing about the score.

Body:

Hi [First name],

Thanks for moving me forward. Before I take the automated assessment, I want to be upfront about how I work, because it affects how that step will go.

I am an AI-native engineer. I do not write code by hand. I direct a fleet of AI agents, I define what gets built and the logic that connects the pieces, and I verify everything before it ships. That is not a shortcut I hide. It is the job itself, and the proof is public. I shipped Xod 1.0.0, an open source AI assistant, solo across Mac, iOS, Android, and watch. I maintain a fleet of published developer tools with roughly 36,000 npm downloads in the last 30 days, and that figure is a partial count. I am a Replit Ambassador in the top 1 percent and I have taken part in 200 plus hackathons. My education is high school. The shipped work is my credential.

A closed assessment with no AI tools measures a workflow I do not use, so a score from it will badly understate what I actually deliver. I have watched that exact gap confuse a process before, and I would rather flag it now than explain it after.

So two questions. First, is AI tooling permitted in your assessment? If it is, I will use my normal setup, openly, the same way I do the real work. Second, if it is not, would you consider an alternative measurement: a real build task, done the way I actually work, with my agents, judged on the shipped result and the verification around it. I am also glad to walk your team through anything I have already shipped, live, and answer for every decision in it.

Either way, I will follow your rules exactly as written. I would rather ask plainly than quietly work around them.

Thanks,
Rene Turcios

## Part C: One-pager: why the sandbox score mismeasures engineers like me

### The short story

Not long ago I went through the hiring process at a major assessment platform, the kind of company whose automated tests much of the industry relies on. My automated score was poor. The recruiter advanced me to a live meeting anyway, and told me why: she wanted to understand how a candidate with my shipped record could fail their own step so badly.

In the live conversation the answer was simple. The test had measured one job: typing code alone, from memory, without tools, against a clock. The work I actually do, well enough to ship a multi-platform product by myself, is a different job: breaking a system into pieces, setting the constraints, directing agents to build it, and verifying the output before it ships. The test and the job were not the same job, so the score described the wrong thing.

### What a closed assessment measures, and what it misses

A closed sandbox measures recall, typing speed, and solo syntax under time pressure. Those are real skills, and they were the job for a long time. They are no longer the whole job.

An engineer who works through agents is doing decomposition, constraint setting, tradeoff judgment, and verification. Those skills decide whether the shipped system is right, not whether the first draft appears quickly. A no-tools test cannot see any of them, so it returns a confident number about a shrinking part of the work. As more engineers shift to directing agents, that gap between the score and the job gets wider, and screen-outs based on the score will keep discarding people who ship.

### What I offer instead

- Permit AI tooling in the assessment and grade the result and the process: the shipped code, the tests, and the record of how it was built.
- Or set a real build task with a real deadline, done with my normal setup, and judge the shipped result and its verification.
- Or skip the proxy entirely and walk through work I have already shipped. I will open any of it live and answer for every decision in it.

### The proof, stated plainly

- Xod 1.0.0: an open source, local-first AI assistant, shipped solo across Mac, iOS, Android, and watch.
- A fleet of published developer tools, in daily use by other engineers.
- Roughly 36,000 npm downloads across my packages in the last 30 days. That is a partial count, and it is the honest one.
- Replit Ambassador, top 1 percent. 200 plus hackathons taken part in, dozens hosted.
- Education: high school. I state that plainly because the record above is the argument, not a credential.

I am not asking for a lower bar. I am asking for the bar to be placed on the work itself.
